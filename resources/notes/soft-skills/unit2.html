Soft Skills and Personality Development

Unit 2: Developing Speaking and Persuasive Writing Skills

1. Speaking

Definition: Speaking is the process of expressing thoughts, ideas and information through spoken words.

Effective speaking requires clear communication, proper pronunciation, suitable pace and confidence.

2. Modes of Speaking

Modes of speaking refer to different ways in which a person communicates through speech.
  • Formal Speaking: Used in professional or official situations.
  • Informal Speaking: Used in casual conversations.
  • Public Speaking: Speaking before an audience.
  • Interactive Speaking: Communication involving participation between speakers and listeners.
Example: Giving a presentation in college is an example of public speaking.

3. Non-Verbal Communication

Definition: Non-verbal communication means communicating without using spoken or written words.

It can include:

  • Facial expressions
  • Eye contact
  • Gestures
  • Posture
  • Body movements
Example: Smiling can show friendliness or happiness without saying anything.

4. Time Management

Definition: Time management is the process of planning and using available time effectively.

Important Points

  • Set clear goals.
  • Prepare a schedule.
  • Prioritize important tasks.
  • Avoid unnecessary delays.
  • Complete work within the given time.
Example: A student divides the day into study, project work and revision time.

5. Preparing Draft

Definition: A draft is an initial version of a speech or written content prepared before the final version.

Steps

  1. Understand the topic.
  2. Collect important points.
  3. Arrange the ideas.
  4. Prepare the first draft.
  5. Check and improve it.
  6. Prepare the final version.

6. Pacing

Definition: Pacing means controlling the speed at which we speak.

Speaking too fast can make the message difficult to understand, while speaking too slowly can reduce the listener's interest.

Example: During a presentation, speak at a comfortable speed and pause at important points.

7. Pronunciation

Definition: Pronunciation is the correct way of saying or producing the sounds of words.

Clear pronunciation makes communication easier to understand.

Example: Practising difficult words before a presentation can improve pronunciation.

8. Practicing

Definition: Practicing means repeatedly performing or rehearsing a task to improve performance.
  • Improves confidence.
  • Reduces nervousness.
  • Improves speaking ability.
  • Helps identify mistakes.

9. Persuasive Writing

Definition: Persuasive writing is writing that attempts to convince the reader to accept an idea, opinion or recommendation.
Example: Writing an article explaining why students should participate in internships.

10. Building an Argument

Definition: Building an argument means presenting a clear claim supported by logical reasons and evidence.

Basic Structure

  1. State the main idea.
  2. Give reasons.
  3. Provide supporting evidence.
  4. Consider other viewpoints.
  5. Give a logical conclusion.

11. Understanding Cultural Context

Definition: Cultural context means understanding the values, beliefs, customs and background of the people for whom we are communicating.

Understanding cultural context helps avoid misunderstanding and makes communication more appropriate.

Example: A communication style that is acceptable in one culture may not have the same meaning in another culture.

12. Using Recommendation

Definition: A recommendation is a suggestion made in favour of a particular action, idea or choice.
Example: "Students should use a study timetable because it helps them manage their time."

13. Using Concrete Evidence in Support

Definition: Concrete evidence means specific facts, examples or information used to support an argument.
Example: Instead of saying "Online learning is useful", provide a specific example or reliable fact to support the statement.

14. Countering Perspectives

Definition: Countering perspectives means considering and responding to an opposing point of view.
Example:

Argument: Online classes save travel time.
Counter Perspective: Offline classes may provide more direct interaction with teachers.

15. Logical Opening, Body and Close

Opening

The opening introduces the topic and attracts the reader's or listener's attention.

Body

The body contains the main arguments, explanations and evidence.

Close

The close summarizes the main idea and provides a conclusion.

Simple Structure:

Opening → Main Argument → Evidence → Counter Perspective → Conclusion

16. Typography and Highlights

Definition: Typography refers to the way text is arranged and presented, while highlights are used to emphasize important information.

Examples

  • Using headings and subheadings.
  • Using bold text for important points.
  • Using bullet points.
  • Highlighting key information.
  • Maintaining proper spacing.
Example: In a report, important headings can be made bold so that readers can easily find them.

Quick Revision

  • Speaking is the expression of ideas through spoken words.
  • Non-verbal communication uses gestures, expressions and body language.
  • Time management helps use time effectively.
  • A draft is the initial version of content.
  • Pacing controls the speed of speaking.
  • Pronunciation means saying words correctly.
  • Persuasive writing tries to convince the reader.
  • Arguments should be supported by reasons and evidence.
  • Counter perspectives consider opposing views.
  • A good persuasive structure has an opening, body and close.
