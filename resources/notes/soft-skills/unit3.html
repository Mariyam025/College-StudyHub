Unit 3: Persuasive Presentation and Self-Assessment

1. Persuasive Presentation

Definition: A persuasive presentation is a presentation designed to convince the audience to accept an idea, opinion or proposed action.

A persuasive presentation uses clear arguments, supporting information and effective communication to influence the audience.

Example: A student gives a presentation explaining why students should participate in internships.

2. Ten-Minute Interactive Argument

Definition: An interactive argument is a presentation in which the presenter communicates with the audience and presents arguments within a limited time.

In a ten-minute presentation, the speaker should organize the important points clearly and use the available time effectively.

Simple Structure

  1. Introduction of the topic
  2. Main argument
  3. Supporting points
  4. Interaction with audience
  5. Conclusion

3. Presentation Slides

Definition: Presentation slides are visual materials used to organize and explain information during a presentation.

Good Slides Should Have

  • Clear headings
  • Short and meaningful points
  • Relevant information
  • Easy-to-read text
  • Proper organization
Example: For a presentation on Artificial Intelligence, slides can contain introduction, applications, advantages, challenges and conclusion.

4. Attracting and Persuading External Audience

Definition: Attracting and persuading an external audience means gaining the attention of people outside the immediate group and presenting arguments that encourage them to consider the proposed idea.

Useful Methods

  • Start with an interesting point.
  • Use clear and simple language.
  • Give relevant examples.
  • Use supporting evidence.
  • Maintain suitable eye contact.
  • Use confident body language.

5. Enacting Change in Strategy

Definition: Enacting change in strategy means changing the approach or method when the existing strategy is not producing the desired result.
Example: If the audience is not responding to a presentation, the speaker may change the way the topic is explained or introduce a practical example.

6. Providing a Course of Action

Definition: A course of action is a clear set of steps suggested to achieve a particular goal.

Example

If the topic is improving college attendance, a possible course of action can be:

  1. Identify reasons for low attendance.
  2. Discuss the problem with students.
  3. Create an attendance plan.
  4. Monitor attendance regularly.

7. Self-Assessment

Definition: Self-assessment is the process of evaluating one's own performance, strengths and areas that need improvement.

After a presentation, the presenter can review their own performance to understand what was effective and what can be improved.

8. Self-Evaluation of Argument

Strength of Argument

Check whether the main argument is logical, clear and supported by appropriate points.

Clarity in Message

Check whether the audience could easily understand the message being communicated.

Structure

Check whether the presentation had a proper beginning, middle and conclusion.

9. Verbal and Non-Verbal Cues

Verbal cues: Words, tone, pronunciation, pace and other features used while speaking.

Non-verbal cues: Eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, posture and body movements.
Example: A speaker may use clear words (verbal cue) and maintain eye contact (non-verbal cue) during a presentation.

10. Getting Peer Evaluation

Definition: Peer evaluation means receiving feedback about performance from classmates or other people at a similar level.

Peer Feedback Can Help Identify

  • Strengths of the presentation
  • Areas for improvement
  • Clarity of the message
  • Speaking style
  • Use of verbal and non-verbal cues

11. Synthesizing Observations

Definition: Synthesizing observations means combining and organizing the observations or feedback collected from different sources to understand overall performance.
Example: After receiving feedback from three classmates, a student identifies common suggestions and uses them to improve the next presentation.

12. SOAR Analysis

SOAR stands for:

S – Strengths
O – Opportunities
A – Aspirations
R – Results

Strengths

Strengths are the positive abilities or qualities that help a person perform effectively.

Opportunities

Opportunities are situations or possibilities that can help a person grow and improve.

Aspirations

Aspirations are the goals or achievements a person wants to reach in the future.

Results

Results are the outcomes achieved through actions and efforts.

Example of SOAR:

Strength: Good communication
Opportunity: College presentations
Aspiration: Become a confident speaker
Result: Better presentation performance

Quick Revision

  • Persuasive presentation aims to convince an audience.
  • Interactive arguments involve audience participation.
  • Presentation slides organize information visually.
  • Self-assessment evaluates one's own performance.
  • Argument strength and message clarity are important during evaluation.
  • Verbal cues involve spoken communication.
  • Non-verbal cues include gestures, posture and eye contact.
  • Peer evaluation provides feedback from others.
  • Synthesizing observations means combining feedback and observations.
  • SOAR stands for Strengths, Opportunities, Aspirations and Results.
