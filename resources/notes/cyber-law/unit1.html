Cyber Law - Unit 1

1. Introduction to Cyber Law

Cyber Law is the area of law that deals with legal issues related to computers, digital technology, computer networks, the Internet and activities carried out in cyberspace.

Simple Definition: Cyber Law provides legal rules for activities and transactions taking place through computers, networks and the Internet.

2. What is Cyberspace?

Cyberspace refers to the interconnected digital environment created by computer networks, communication systems, websites, applications and Internet-connected devices.

3. Need for Cyber Law

The rapid growth of the Internet has created new opportunities as well as new legal problems. Cyber law provides a legal framework for dealing with many of these problems.

  • Prevention and punishment of cybercrime
  • Protection of digital information
  • Protection of privacy
  • Recognition of electronic records
  • Recognition of electronic transactions
  • Protection of intellectual property
  • Regulation of online activities

4. Objectives of Cyber Law

  • Provide legal recognition to electronic transactions.
  • Control and address cybercrime.
  • Protect users and organizations.
  • Support secure electronic communication.
  • Protect digital intellectual property.
  • Provide legal mechanisms for resolving cyber disputes.

5. Cybercrime

Cybercrime refers to illegal activities in which computers, computer networks or digital systems are used as a target, tool or environment.

6. Examples of Cybercrime

  • Hacking
  • Phishing
  • Identity theft
  • Online fraud
  • Cyber stalking
  • Data theft
  • Malware attacks
  • Unauthorized access
  • Denial-of-service attacks
  • Online impersonation

7. Cybercrime Against Individuals

These crimes target individual users.

  • Identity theft
  • Online cheating
  • Cyber stalking
  • Phishing
  • Online harassment
  • Privacy violations

8. Cybercrime Against Organizations

Organizations may become targets of cyberattacks intended to steal data, disrupt services or obtain unauthorized access.

  • Data breaches
  • Ransomware attacks
  • Unauthorized access
  • Denial-of-service attacks
  • Corporate data theft

9. Cybercrime Against Government

Government systems can be targeted to steal information, disrupt services or compromise sensitive systems.

10. Cyber Ethics

Cyber ethics refers to principles of responsible and acceptable behaviour while using computers, digital systems and the Internet.

Examples of Cyber Ethics

  • Respect other people's privacy.
  • Do not access accounts without permission.
  • Do not spread false information intentionally.
  • Do not steal digital content.
  • Do not distribute malware.
  • Respect copyright.
  • Use strong security practices.

11. Cyber Law and Cyber Ethics

Cyber Law Cyber Ethics
Based on legal rules Based on ethical principles
Violation may result in legal consequences Violation may be socially or professionally unacceptable
Enforced through legal mechanisms Guides responsible behaviour

12. Intellectual Property

Intellectual Property (IP) refers to creations of the human mind that receive legal protection under applicable laws.

Examples

  • Copyright
  • Patents
  • Trademarks
  • Trade secrets

13. Copyright

Copyright protects original creative works such as literary works, software, music, photographs and other eligible works.

14. Patent

A patent provides legal protection for qualifying inventions for a limited period subject to the applicable law.

15. Trademark

A trademark is a sign, name, symbol or other identifier that distinguishes the goods or services of one business from those of others.

16. Data Protection

Data protection refers to measures and legal principles designed to protect personal and other sensitive information from unauthorized access, misuse, loss or disclosure.

17. Privacy

Privacy is the ability of individuals to have appropriate control over information about themselves and how it is collected, used or disclosed.

18. Importance of Data Privacy

  • Protects personal information.
  • Reduces risk of identity theft.
  • Builds user trust.
  • Supports responsible data handling.
  • Reduces the impact of unauthorized disclosure.

19. Electronic Record

An electronic record is information created, stored or transmitted in electronic form.

Examples include emails, electronic documents, digital invoices and computer-generated records.

20. Digital Signature

A digital signature is a cryptographic mechanism used to authenticate the origin and integrity of an electronic document or message.

Purposes of Digital Signature

  • Authentication
  • Integrity
  • Non-repudiation

21. Digital Signature vs Electronic Signature

Digital Signature Electronic Signature
Uses cryptographic techniques Broader category of electronic indication of agreement/signing
Can provide integrity and authentication Security properties depend on the method used

22. Cyber Jurisdiction

Cyber jurisdiction refers to the authority of a court or legal system to deal with activities, persons or disputes involving cyberspace.

23. Challenges of Cyber Jurisdiction

  • Internet activities can cross national borders.
  • Users and servers may be located in different countries.
  • Identifying responsible persons can be difficult.
  • Different countries may have different laws.

24. Electronic Commerce

Electronic commerce, or e-commerce, refers to buying and selling goods or services using electronic networks.

Examples

  • Online shopping
  • Online banking
  • Online ticket booking
  • Digital payments

25. E-Governance

E-governance means the use of information and communication technologies by government organizations to provide services and information.

26. Benefits of E-Governance

  • Faster services
  • Improved accessibility
  • Better information availability
  • Reduced paperwork
  • Greater transparency in appropriate processes

27. Important Terms

Term Meaning

Cybercrime Illegal activity involving computers or digital networks
Cyber Law Law relating to digital and online activities
Privacy Protection and appropriate control of personal information
Digital Signature Cryptographic method for authentication and integrity

28. Unit 1 Quick Revision

  • Cyber law deals with legal issues related to cyberspace.
  • Cybercrime involves illegal activities involving digital systems.
  • Cyber ethics promotes responsible online behaviour.
  • Intellectual property protects eligible creations and inventions.
  • Data protection focuses on appropriate handling and protection of data.
  • Digital signatures use cryptographic techniques.
  • Cyber jurisdiction deals with legal authority in cyberspace.
