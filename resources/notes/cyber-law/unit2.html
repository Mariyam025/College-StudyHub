Cyber Law - Unit 2

1. Evolution of Cybercrime

Cybercrime developed along with the growth of computers, computer networks and the Internet. As digital technology became widely used, criminals also found new ways to misuse these technologies.

Cybercrime can involve attacks against individuals, organizations, businesses and government systems.

2. Cyber Fraud

Cyber fraud is the use of digital technologies to deceive a person or organization for unlawful financial or other gain.

Examples

  • Fake online shopping websites
  • Phishing messages
  • Payment scams
  • Identity-based fraud
  • Fake investment schemes

3. Cyber Cheating

Cyber cheating involves intentionally deceiving someone through digital communication or online services.

4. Phishing

Phishing is a social-engineering technique in which attackers attempt to trick people into revealing sensitive information or performing an unwanted action.

Example: A fake message may claim to be from a bank and ask the user to click a link and enter account credentials.

Protection Against Phishing

  • Check the sender carefully.
  • Do not click suspicious links.
  • Verify websites before entering sensitive information.
  • Use multi-factor authentication where available.
  • Do not share passwords or OTPs.

5. Email Spoofing

Email spoofing occurs when an email is made to appear as if it came from a different sender than the actual sender.

Spoofed emails may be used in phishing and fraud.

6. Email Bombing

Email bombing refers to sending an extremely large number of emails to a target address or system with the intention of overwhelming it or causing disruption.

7. Cyber Stalking

Cyber stalking refers to repeated unwanted monitoring, communication or harassment of a person using digital technologies.

Examples

  • Repeated unwanted messages
  • Persistent online monitoring
  • Threatening communication
  • Repeated harassment through social media

8. Denial of Service Attack

A Denial of Service (DoS) attack attempts to make a service or resource unavailable to legitimate users by overwhelming or disrupting it.

9. Distributed Denial of Service

A Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attack uses traffic or requests from multiple systems to overwhelm a target.

10. DoS vs DDoS

DoS DDoS
Attack traffic may originate from a single source or limited set Attack traffic comes from multiple distributed sources
Generally simpler structure Generally more difficult to block because sources are distributed

11. Cyber Terrorism

Cyber terrorism is a term used for serious cyber activities intended to support or cause political or ideological violence, intimidation or major disruption.

Because the term has different legal and policy definitions, its exact scope depends on the applicable law and context.

12. Salami Attack

A salami attack refers to a fraud technique in which very small amounts or changes are taken or made repeatedly so that each individual change may appear insignificant.

13. Data Diddling

Data diddling means unauthorized alteration of data before, during or after data processing.

Example: Changing a transaction amount in a computer system before it is processed.

14. Web Jacking

Web jacking refers to gaining unauthorized control over or redirecting a website or web resource.

15. Internet Time Theft

Internet time theft refers to unauthorized use of another person's Internet account or access resources.

16. Malware

Malware means malicious software designed to disrupt, damage, spy on, modify or gain unauthorized access to computer systems or data.

Common Types of Malware

  • Virus
  • Worm
  • Trojan horse
  • Ransomware
  • Spyware
  • Adware

17. Virus

A computer virus is malicious code that attaches itself to a host file or program and may replicate when the infected host is executed.

18. Worm

A worm is malware capable of self-replication and can spread between systems, often through networks, without needing to attach to another program in the same way as a traditional virus.

19. Trojan Horse

A Trojan horse is malicious software that is presented or disguised as legitimate or useful software.

20. Ransomware

Ransomware is malware that prevents access to systems or data, commonly by encrypting files, and demands payment or other action from the victim.

21. Spyware

Spyware is software designed to monitor or collect information about users or their activities without appropriate authorization.

22. Cyber Pornography

Cyber pornography refers to unlawful or prohibited pornographic activities or content involving digital technologies. Applicable laws differ by jurisdiction and by the nature of the material.

23. Intellectual Property Crimes

Intellectual property crimes involve unauthorized use, copying, distribution or other infringement of legally protected intellectual property.

Examples

  • Software piracy
  • Unauthorized copying of digital content
  • Trademark misuse
  • Copyright infringement

24. Web Defamation

Web defamation refers to unlawful publication of defamatory material using websites, social media or other online platforms, subject to the applicable law.

25. Identity Theft

Identity theft occurs when someone obtains and uses another person's personal information without authorization, often for fraud or other unlawful purposes.

26. Common Information Targeted in Identity Theft

  • Name and address
  • Login credentials
  • Financial information
  • Government identification details
  • Personal account information

27. Online Gambling

Online gambling involves betting or wagering through Internet-based platforms. Its legality depends on the jurisdiction and the specific activity.

28. Cybercrime Prevention

  • Use strong and unique passwords.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication.
  • Keep operating systems and applications updated.
  • Use reputable security software.
  • Back up important data.
  • Avoid suspicious links and attachments.
  • Use secure networks.
  • Protect personal information.

29. Cyber Security Awareness

Cyber security awareness means understanding digital risks and knowing how to use technology safely and responsibly.

30. Cybercrime Reporting

When a person becomes a victim of cybercrime, useful evidence may include messages, emails, transaction records, screenshots and relevant website information. Reporting should be made through the appropriate official cybercrime or law-enforcement channel.

31. Cybercrime vs Traditional Crime

Feature Cybercrime Traditional Crime
Environment Digital/cyberspace Primarily physical environment
Geographical reach Can cross borders quickly Often more geographically constrained
Evidence Often digital Often physical, though digital evidence may also exist

DDoS Distributed denial-of-service attack
Data Diddling Unauthorized alteration of data
Web Jacking Unauthorized control or redirection of a website
Identity Theft Unauthorized use of another person's identity information

33. Unit 2 Quick Revision

  • Phishing uses deception to obtain information or cause an action.
  • Email spoofing makes an email appear to come from another sender.
  • DoS attempts to make a service unavailable.
  • DDoS uses distributed sources.
  • Malware includes viruses, worms, Trojans, ransomware and spyware.
  • Data diddling means unauthorized alteration of data.
  • Identity theft involves unauthorized use of another person's identity information.
  • Cybercrime prevention depends on technical controls and user awareness.
