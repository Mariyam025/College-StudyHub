Artificial Intelligence - Unit 1

1. Introduction to Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a branch of computer science concerned with building systems that can perform tasks that normally require human-like intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, perception, language processing and decision-making.

Simple Definition: AI enables machines or computer programs to perform intelligent tasks.

2. Examples of Artificial Intelligence

  • Voice assistants
  • Recommendation systems
  • Face recognition
  • Chatbots
  • Fraud detection
  • Medical diagnosis support
  • Self-driving technologies
  • Spam filtering
  • Language translation

3. Goals of Artificial Intelligence

  • Develop intelligent machines.
  • Enable machines to learn from data.
  • Enable machines to reason and solve problems.
  • Understand human language.
  • Recognize images, speech and patterns.
  • Support decision-making.
  • Automate complex tasks.

4. Characteristics of AI

  • Learning ability
  • Reasoning
  • Problem solving
  • Knowledge representation
  • Perception
  • Language understanding
  • Decision making

5. AI vs Traditional Computer Program

Feature Traditional Program AI System
Decision making Mostly predefined rules Can use reasoning or learned patterns
Learning Usually does not learn automatically Some AI systems can learn from data
Problem type Well-defined problems Can handle complex or uncertain problems
Adaptability Usually limited Can be designed to adapt

6. Applications of Artificial Intelligence

Healthcare

AI can assist in medical image analysis, patient monitoring, drug discovery and clinical decision support.

Education

AI can provide personalized learning, automated feedback and intelligent tutoring.

Banking

AI is used for fraud detection, risk analysis and customer support.

Transportation

AI can be used in route planning, traffic prediction and driver-assistance systems.

Entertainment

Recommendation systems can suggest movies, songs and other content based on user behaviour.

Agriculture

AI can support crop monitoring, disease detection and yield prediction.

7. Types of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Narrow Intelligence

Narrow AI is designed to perform a specific task or a limited set of tasks. Most practical AI applications today fall into this category.

Examples: recommendation systems, spam filters and voice recognition systems.

Artificial General Intelligence

AGI refers to a hypothetical form of AI capable of performing a broad range of intellectual tasks at a human-like level.

Artificial Superintelligence

ASI is a hypothetical concept referring to an AI whose general intellectual capabilities would greatly exceed those of humans.

8. AI Based on Functionality

Reactive Machines

Reactive systems respond to current inputs without maintaining a general memory of past experiences.

Limited Memory

These systems can use some information from previous observations or stored data when making decisions.

Theory of Mind

This is a research concept involving AI systems that would understand mental states such as beliefs, intentions and emotions.

Self-Aware AI

Self-aware AI is a hypothetical concept involving systems with awareness of their own internal state.

9. Intelligent Agent

An intelligent agent is a system that perceives its environment through sensors and takes actions through actuators to achieve a goal.

Example: A robot uses cameras and sensors to understand its environment and motors to move.

10. Components of an Intelligent Agent

  • Environment: The world in which the agent operates.
  • Sensors: Collect information from the environment.
  • Agent program: Processes percepts and selects actions.
  • Actuators: Perform actions on the environment.

11. Rational Agent

A rational agent selects an action that is expected to achieve the best result according to its performance measure and available information.

12. PEAS

PEAS is a framework used to describe the task environment of an intelligent agent.

  • P - Performance Measure
  • E - Environment
  • A - Actuators
  • S - Sensors

Example: Self-Driving Car

PEAS Component Example
Performance Safety, speed, comfort
Environment Roads, vehicles, pedestrians
Actuators Steering, brakes, accelerator
Sensors Cameras, radar, GPS

13. Types of Agents

  • Simple reflex agent
  • Model-based reflex agent
  • Goal-based agent
  • Utility-based agent
  • Learning agent

14. Simple Reflex Agent

A simple reflex agent selects actions based mainly on the current percept using condition-action rules.

Example: If temperature is high, turn on the cooling system.

15. Model-Based Agent

A model-based agent maintains an internal representation of relevant aspects of the environment to help make decisions.

16. Goal-Based Agent

A goal-based agent chooses actions by considering whether they help achieve a specified goal.

17. Utility-Based Agent

A utility-based agent evaluates possible outcomes using a utility measure and chooses an action expected to provide a desirable result.

18. Learning Agent

A learning agent improves its performance by learning from experience.

19. Components of Learning Agent

  • Learning element
  • Performance element
  • Critic
  • Problem generator

20. Turing Test

The Turing Test was proposed by Alan Turing as a way of discussing whether a machine can exhibit behaviour that is indistinguishable from human conversation under the test conditions.

21. Advantages of AI

  • Automation of repetitive tasks
  • Fast processing
  • Pattern recognition
  • Decision support
  • 24-hour operation for suitable systems
  • Can process large amounts of data

22. Limitations of AI

  • Development can be expensive.
  • Requires data and computational resources.
  • AI systems can make incorrect predictions.
  • Some systems are difficult to explain.
  • AI does not automatically possess human common sense.

23. AI and Human Intelligence

Feature Human Intelligence Artificial Intelligence
Learning Can learn from varied experiences Depends on system design and data
Emotion Has emotions and subjective experiences Does not inherently have human emotions
Speed Limited by human processing Can process some tasks very quickly
Adaptability Broad and flexible Depends on capabilities and training

24. Unit 1 Quick Revision

  • AI is a branch of computer science concerned with intelligent behaviour.
  • An intelligent agent perceives its environment and acts upon it.
  • PEAS stands for Performance, Environment, Actuators and Sensors.
  • Narrow AI is designed for specific tasks.
  • A rational agent selects actions according to its performance measure.
  • Learning agents improve using experience.
