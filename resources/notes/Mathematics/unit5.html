1. Moments

Moments are numerical values used to describe important characteristics of a distribution such as central tendency, variation, skewness and kurtosis.

r-th raw moment:

μ'r = Σfxr / Σf
r-th central moment:

μr = Σf(x - x̄)r / Σf
Example:

If the values are 1, 2, 3, then moments can be calculated using their deviations from the mean.

2. Important Moments

Moment Meaning
First Moment Related to mean
Second Moment Related to variance
Third Moment Used for skewness
Fourth Moment Used for kurtosis

3. Moment Generating Function

A Moment Generating Function (MGF) is a function used to generate moments of a probability distribution.

MX(t) = E(etX)

The r-th moment can be obtained by differentiating the MGF r times and putting t = 0.

μ'r = M(r)(0)
Simple idea:

First differentiate the MGF.
Then put t = 0.
The result gives the required moment.

4. Skewness

Skewness measures the degree of asymmetry of a distribution around its mean.

Type Meaning
Positive Skewness Tail is longer towards the right
Negative Skewness Tail is longer towards the left
Zero Skewness Distribution is approximately symmetric
Moment coefficient of skewness:

β1 = μ32 / μ23

5. Kurtosis

Kurtosis measures the degree of peakedness or flatness of a distribution.

β2 = μ4 / μ22
Type Condition
Mesokurtic β₂ = 3
Leptokurtic β₂ > 3
Platykurtic β₂ < 3

6. Correlation

Correlation measures the degree and direction of relationship between two variables.

Example:

Study time and examination marks may have a positive relationship: generally, as study time increases, marks may also increase.

7. Types of Correlation

Type Meaning
Positive Correlation Both variables move in the same direction
Negative Correlation Variables move in opposite directions
Zero Correlation No linear relationship

8. Karl Pearson's Correlation Coefficient

Karl Pearson's coefficient measures the linear relationship between two variables.

r = Σ(x - x̄)(y - ȳ) / √[Σ(x - x̄)² × Σ(y - ȳ)²]

The value of r lies between -1 and +1.

-1 ≤ r ≤ +1

9. Regression Analysis

Regression analysis is used to study the relationship between variables and to estimate the value of one variable from another variable.

Example:

If we know the advertising expenditure, regression can be used to estimate expected sales.

10. Regression Lines

There are two regression lines:

  • Regression of Y on X
  • Regression of X on Y
Regression equation of Y on X:

Y - ȳ = byx(X - x̄)
Regression equation of X on Y:

X - x̄ = bxy(Y - ȳ)

11. Correlation vs Regression

Correlation Regression
Measures relationship Used for estimation/prediction
Symmetric relationship Distinguishes dependent and independent variables
Coefficient is r Uses regression coefficients
